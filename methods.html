How this analysis works

Methods and interpretation for the 4,116-burst PSP/FIELDS TDS atlas. These are exploratory statistical descriptions, with no supplied plasma-wave or dust-impact labels.

“Phase baseline × burst gain”: the orange count curve

This means a typical scan-dependent count pattern, rescaled to the total counts in the selected burst. The atlas now labels it Scan-pattern reference, scaled to this burst.

“Phase” here is elapsed time since the SWEAP sweep started, using SWEAP_Start and time within the burst. It is not the phase of a voltage or magnetic oscillation, and is not an angle in radians. “Gain” is a fitted numerical scale factor; it is not a measured hardware gain, detector efficiency, or calibration correction.

1. Learn a reference pattern from other days

We sum the digital counts into 128-sample bins: 66.667 µs per bin at 1.92 MSa/s. There are 256 such bins in each 17.067 ms burst. For bin j, its sweep-time coordinate is:

sⱼ = SWEAP_Start + (j + ½) × 128 / sample_rate

For the day being examined, the reference uses records from other days with the same SWEAP status code. The observed sweep-time range is divided into 600 cells. Within each cell we take the median of the count-bin values. Empty cells are filled by linear interpolation, a three-cell median smoother is applied, and the profile is interpolated to the selected burst’s bin centers. Interpolation beyond sampled endpoints uses the nearest endpoint value. We impose no periodic wrapping or assumed sweep frequency.

This supplies an unscaled reference bⱼ, in counts per 66.667 µs bin. The selected day supplies none of the training counts. At least 20 other-day records with matching status are required; this is a practical support rule, not a guarantee that every sweep-time cell is well sampled. With fewer records, the display uses a flagged cross-status reference, which is excluded from the held-out score and residual-count classification. There are 5 such bursts in this version.

2. Scale the reference to this burst’s overall level

Let C = Σcⱼ be the observed total and B = Σbⱼ the total predicted by the unscaled profile. The implemented scale factor and orange curve are:

g = (C + 1) / (B + 1)
orange reference: eⱼ = g × bⱼ

The added 1 prevents division by zero and stabilizes very small totals. Consequently the reference total matches the observed total approximately, rather than exactly. For example, if B = 100 and C = 200, g = 201/101 ≈ 1.990 and the reference total becomes ≈199.01 counts. The entire curve is multiplied by the same factor; its shape and timing are not fitted separately to each burst.

The shape comes from other days; the overall scale uses this burst’s observed counts. Thus the orange curve is a fitted reference for comparing shapes, not an independent prediction of this burst’s total. If a physical process increases counts uniformly throughout a burst, this normalization can absorb that increase into g.

3. Examine departures from that reference

displayed residual: rⱼ = (cⱼ − eⱼ) / √(eⱼ + 1)

Positive residuals mean more counts than the scaled reference; negative residuals mean fewer. The square-root denominator is a convenient count-dependent normalization. It is not a calibrated uncertainty or a significance in standard deviations: scan structure, detector behavior, estimation error and correlated samples are not described by a validated noise model here.

The reported held-out-day R² (0.743) compares log(1 + C) with log(1 + B) using the unscaled reference, on supported matching-status cases. It does not use g. This separates the independent prediction score from the burst-normalized orange display.

What this reference can—and cannot—remove

It removes a typical dependence on sweep timing and an overall burst count scale. It can retain fine scan structure, timing drift, changing plasma conditions and selection effects. Conversely, a physical feature that repeatedly occurs at the same sweep timing could enter the reference. The status bits and detailed electron energy/angle sequence have not been decoded. The curve is empirical, not a physical SPAN-electron response model.

Input data and displayed signals

The current 16 CDF files contain 4,116 bursts. Each provides 32,768 samples at 1.92 MSa/s. Only those actual samples are analyzed; padded array elements are excluded. The analog channels are antenna voltages in engineering mV and a search-coil signal in engineering nT. Frequency-response-corrected physical-unit flags are off. Antenna voltages are not converted into electric fields.

V4/SCM5 and V5/SCM4 configurations are kept separate. Missing channels are explicitly marked absent. Saturated records remain visible, but do not enter analog fits or the waveform-association screens. Digital counts are nonnegative integers; every loaded waveform sum was checked against its CDF burst total.

Full-record atlas traces use min/max envelopes in 256 display bins. Their apparent ripples are not resolved waveforms. The zoom contains 512 consecutive actual samples. Example figures use 1024-sample zooms. Voltages and SCM have their channel medians removed for display. The original CDFs are unchanged.

How waveform groups are formed

The primary analog representation has 89 descriptors of waveform shape: crest factor and fourth moment, asymmetry, temporal energy concentration and duration, envelope occupancy, zero crossings, autocorrelation, spectral entropy and frequency distribution, channel correlations, envelope correlations, and weighted spectral coherence. Five absolute log-RMS amplitudes are retained separately, rather than included in the primary fit.

Features use median/IQR scaling, with a standard-deviation fallback; standardized values are clipped to ±5. Temporal, spectral and cross-channel feature families receive equal total squared-distance weight. Ward hierarchical clustering operates in this full feature space. The two-dimensional PCA plot is for navigation only.

We test 2–10 groups and choose the highest mean silhouette among solutions with at least 20 records per group. The current primary choice is 2 groups. This is a declared browsing rule, not evidence that the solar wind has exactly 2 physical populations. The alternate configuration has a separate 2–4-group search, with equal per-feature weights and the same minimum group size; it can remain an unpartitioned pool.

Core agreement is not a classification probability. Group membership can change substantially as files are added; event IDs remain stable. Sensitivity checks change the spectral representation, remove SCM features, add absolute amplitude, change linkage, and fit dates separately.

Digital groups use count dispersion, temporal concentration, entropy and spectral descriptors, with a minimum of 32 total counts and 40 resamples. Residual groups use departures from the scan reference. The joint partition combines analog features, raw count shape and count/analog envelope relationships. G, A, C, R and J numbers belong to separate analyses; equal numbers do not imply equal classes.

Electron-count and voltage correlations

Wave cycles and wave envelopes are separate questions. The native-sample diagnostic compares signed voltage with count increments at 1.92 MSa/s (0.521 µs spacing). Zero-lag Pearson correlation tests simultaneous sample association; frequency-resolved magnitude-squared coherence can expose a shared oscillation despite a phase offset. The current catalog stores peak coherence and its frequency, not a calibrated phase lag. A null zero-lag coefficient does not rule out cycle-scale association.

The envelope diagnostic compares 128-sample RMS voltage with binned counts or their scan-reference residuals, at 66.667 µs resolution. It measures amplitude modulation, not individual high-frequency cycles. Signed voltage averaged over those bins is an additional coarse diagnostic and must not be interpreted as the native waveform. Sparse counts, scan structure, instrumental lines and unknown relative timing affect interpretation; native sampling cadence alone does not establish the electron channel’s effective response bandwidth. These diagnostics are reported separately and are not detections of wave–particle coupling.

Across bursts: compare total electron counts with each voltage’s full-record RMS using Spearman rank correlation. An adjusted rank correlation removes fitted dependence on day, SWEAP status, TWTA flag, selection type, radius rank and a cubic spline of sweep time with five interior knots. This is descriptive adjustment, not a causal model or proof that all confounding has been removed.

Within a burst: compare count-bin totals and scan-reference residuals with voltage RMS envelopes and signed voltage means, using the same 128-sample bins. The signed means represent a coarse, boxcar-averaged voltage trace; they do not resolve the high-frequency waveform phase. Correlations are computed on overlapping segments across ±16 bins (±1.067 ms), recentering each overlap. Positive lag means counts follow voltage.

Candidate rankings search four envelopes, four signed means and 33 lags; the largest coefficient is therefore selected upward. Linear detrending and first-difference checks expose sensitivity to slow changes. Circular shifts and other bursts matched approximately by day, status, sweep timing and count level supply exploratory comparisons, whose exchangeability assumptions can fail. Direct 1.92 MSa/s correlations and Welch coherence are also retained in the coefficient table.

What the CDF tells us about SPAN-e operation

All 16 input CDFs were inspected. SWEAP_Status is described as a coded SWEAP status word; SWEAP_Electron_Mask and SWEAP_Ion_Mask are coded anode-selection masks. SWEAP_Start is the time since the last SWEAP sweep start, in seconds. These fields provide operating-state and sweep-timing context, but their variable attributes do not define the status bits or map them to named SPAN-e modes.

The electron mask is 0xFFFFFFFF and the ion mask is 0x0002000A in every burst. The electron mask has all 32 bits set; mapping those bits to detector/anode selections requires its encoding definition. Sweep-start offsets range from approximately 57.45 µs to 0.873806 s. The rare status 0x8181C4BF differs from 0x818144BF by bit 15 (zero-based); this is a bit comparison, not a mode interpretation.

The scan-reference model already conditions on exact status word when supported by sufficient other-day data. We have not decoded a per-sample energy/angle sequence or named operating mode. A SWEAP-to-FIELDS status/interface definition, or time-matched SPAN-e housekeeping with documented mode fields, is needed for that interpretation. Inspected attributes and value inventory.

SCM–voltage associations and persistent lines

Each available voltage is compared with its simultaneously recorded SCM channel. Spectra and cross-spectra use Hann-window Welch estimates with 4096 samples, 50% overlap and 468.75 Hz frequency spacing: 15 overlapping segments per burst. Magnitude-squared coherence is averaged over 1–864 kHz with weights proportional to √(voltage PSD × SCM PSD).

Persistent-line masks are learned separately for the two configurations. A peak must exceed a local 31-bin median by 8 dB in at least 60% of eligible records, and lie above approximately 7.5 kHz. We exclude the peak and its neighboring Welch bins when computing the outside-line metric. This changes both the contributing bins and their weights; it does not filter the displayed waveform.

Without transfer-function phase correction and verified relative timing, these comparisons do not establish polarization, propagation, E/B impedance or Poynting flux.

Interpretation, provenance and limitations

“Without physics priors” means that no physical class labels or event templates were supplied. Feature choices, distance metrics and thresholds still impose statistical assumptions. Onboard selection means this catalog describes recorded bursts, not unbiased occurrence rates. A single burst can contain several different structures.

Solar distance comes from the CDF spacecraft-reported distance and validity flags; full SPICE state vectors have not been loaded. Radius, direction, date and instrument state were withheld from primary clustering and retained as context.

Verification includes source SHA-256 hashes, record identities and timestamps, available-channel checks, finite waveform values, exact count totals, coefficient bounds, synthetic lag/coherence checks, embedded preview decoding, and JavaScript checks with a mocked DOM. The actual browser rendering has not been tested by automated browser interaction.
